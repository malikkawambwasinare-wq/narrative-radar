# Competitive Scan: Does anyone map "competing explanations for the same question"?

**Bottom line:** No. As of August 2026, nothing on the market renders "the question is the durable object; answers compete for it" as a navigable consumer surface — and *nothing anywhere* attaches per-answer accountability receipts (prediction ledgers, deadline slippage, recycled share) to the nodes of such a map. Every existing product maps one adjacent thing: coverage plurality (Ground News), argument structure (Kialo/Society Library), evidence direction (scite/Consensus), probability (Metaculus/Rootclaim), question demand (AnswerThePublic), or narrative threat (Blackbird.AI). The 360 view sits at an empty intersection of all six.

---

## 1. Category-by-category findings

### A. News plurality tools — Ground News, AllSides, Verity (Improve the News)

- **What they map:** The same *event* covered by outlets across a left–center–right axis. Ground News's signature "Blindspot" flags stories disproportionately covered by one side; users get a personal bias-distribution graph ([Ground News](https://ground.news/), [help docs](https://help.ground.news/en/articles/485057), [rating system](https://ground.news/rating-system)). Verity (ex-"Improve the News", Max Tegmark's MIT-born nonprofit, rebranded 2023) adds "nuance" and "establishment" sliders and explicitly separates "facts from narratives" ([Verity about](https://www.verity.news/about), [FAQ](https://www.improvethenews.org/faq), [StarTalk interview](https://startalkmedia.com/show/improving-the-news-with-max-tegmark/)).
- **What works:** The blindspot framing is emotionally sticky — "here's what your side isn't showing you" is a strong hook, and it proved a consumer subscription market exists for epistemic tooling (Ground News is one of the few profitable indie news apps, sustained by relentless YouTube-sponsor marketing).
- **What users ignore/mock:** The ubiquitous YouTube sponsor reads have made it a meme; the deeper critique is that a **single left–right axis is the only dimension** — it maps *who is covering a story*, never *which explanation is true or aging well*. Verity's sliders are widely praised in interviews and barely used in practice; slider-tuning is homework.
- **What they don't have:** No competing-explanations structure at all (an event is not a question), no predictions, no receipts, no memory — yesterday's blindspot vanishes.

### B. AI answer engines — Perplexity, ChatGPT, Gemini, AI Overviews

- **What they map:** Nothing persistent. They synthesize one linear answer per query. Perplexity's single-answer focus is itself cited as restrictive for research use ([alternatives roundups](https://storyflow.so/blog/best-perplexity-alternatives-2026), [Windows Forum](https://windowsforum.com/windows-news.4/perplexity-alternatives-2026-citations-safety-privacy-and-enterprise-ai.404807/)). No engine has a dedicated "rival explanations" mode ([2026 answer-engine comparisons](https://everything-pr.com/the-five-answer-engines-2026-chatgpt-claude-gemini-perplexity-google-ai-overviews-compared)).
- **Critical 2026 datapoint:** Google actually *tried* perspective-surfacing twice and retreated twice. The "Perspectives" filter was replaced by a "Forums" filter in March 2024 ([Search Engine Land](https://searchengineland.com/google-to-replace-perspectives-filter-with-forums-filter-438473)), and the AI Overviews feature "What People Suggest" — which clustered diverse health anecdotes into themes, essentially a mini 360-view for health questions — was **entirely scrapped in March 2026** as part of results-page simplification ([Newsgram](https://www.newsgram.com/science/2026/03/28/google-just-switched-off-its-ai-overview-feature)).
- **What's missing:** LLM answers produce an "illusion of consensus" (the exact criticism leveled at Grokipedia in [PNAS](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.2603294123)): statistical text prediction masks uncertainty and minority positions. No persistence, no track record, no falsifiability. **Strategic read:** answer engines are the *rabbit hole's replacement, not its exit* — they deepen whichever answer the query presupposes ("adrenal fatigue symptoms" gets an adrenal-fatigue answer).

### C. Wikipedia and Grokipedia

- **What they map:** Wikipedia's NPOV renders competing views *in prose, weighted by source proportion* ([NPOV policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Neutral_point_of_view)); editors are told to avoid dedicated "Controversy" sections ([Wikipedia:Criticism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Criticism)). Grokipedia (xAI, Oct 2025) generates single AI texts with "no visible record of competing claims or editorial reasoning" and fewer references per word ([PNAS divergence study](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.2603294123), [Tom's Guide on its delay/relaunch turbulence](https://www.tomsguide.com/ai/what-is-grokipedia-elon-musk-just-delayed-his-wikipedia-rival-heres-why)).
- **What works:** Wikipedia's controversy articles are the closest thing normal people have to a 360 view, and they get enormous traffic.
- **What's missing:** Prose, not navigable structure; camps aren't first-class objects; no predictor identities, no dated prediction ledger, no scoring, no lineage ("adrenal fatigue" and "mitochondrial dysfunction" are two unlinked articles, not one question with a succession of answers).

### D. Forecasting platforms — Metaculus, Long Bets, PunditTracker

- **What they map:** Metaculus "question groups" collect closely related *forecast* questions on one page so predictions stay coordinated ([FAQ](https://www.metaculus.com/faq/)); conditional questions exist. Long Bets holds money-backed long-term predictions to public resolution ([longbets.org](https://longbets.org/)). PunditTracker scored 120 named pundits A–F on accuracy plus boldness — **and died circa 2014** ([Ritholtz](https://ritholtz.com/2013/02/pundit-tracker/), [Significance journal](https://rss.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/j.1740-9713.2013.00675.x)).
- **What works:** This tradition *owns the receipt*: resolution criteria, dated claims, scoring. Question groups prove clustering-by-underlying-driver is feasible.
- **What's missing:** Questions are clustered by *operational relatedness*, not by "which worldview does this resolve for/against." No mapping from a forecast to the *narratives* that live or die by it — nobody renders "10yr Treasury above 5%" as the shared clock of two distinct narratives. Audience is a small rationalist niche; the surface is a data table, not a map. PunditTracker's death is the key cautionary tale: **accountability alone, without a discovery/navigation use case, had no retention loop.** Narrative Radar's answer to that grave is that receipts are attached to a map people consult for orientation, not a leaderboard they visit once.

### E. Bayesian competing-hypotheses analysis — Rootclaim, ACH

- **What they map:** Rootclaim explicitly enumerates rival hypotheses for one question (COVID origins, chemical attacks) and assigns probabilities via Bayesian likelihood chains; it staged the $100k lab-leak debate ([Rootclaim challenge](https://www.rootclaim.com/rootclaim-challenge), [Scott Alexander's review](https://www.astralcodexten.com/p/practically-a-book-review-rootclaim), [Rootclaim's response](https://blog.rootclaim.com/covid-origins-debate-response-to-scott-alexander/)). ACH is the CIA's 50-year-old structured method — hypotheses × evidence consistency matrix, PARC free tool ([SOS Intelligence explainer](https://sosintel.co.uk/mastering-the-analysis-of-competing-hypotheses-ach-a-practical-framework-for-clear-thinking/)).
- **What works:** This is the intellectually correct skeleton — the question is the unit, answers compete, evidence discriminates. ACH proves the *method* survives 50 years of institutional use.
- **What users ignore/mock:** Rootclaim became the story of its own overconfidence — it lost the debate, then *raised* its lab-leak probability, which critics read as unfalsifiability wearing a math costume. Coverage is a handful of analyses after years; each costs expert-weeks. ACH software is analyst homework — zero consumer form factor.
- **What's missing:** Scale, freshness, named-predictor accountability (it scores hypotheses, not the people selling them), and any notion of narrative ecology (supplements, monetization, mutation).

### F. Argument mapping — Kialo, DebateGraph, Arguman, Society Library

- **What they map:** Kialo: pro/con trees on debate theses, used mainly in classrooms ([kialo-edu research page](https://www.kialo-edu.com/research)). DebateGraph: collaborative policy-issue maps used by the White House and CNN circa 2010 ([Denison writeup](https://denison.edu/academics/teaching-center/feature/91370)). The Society Library: the most serious effort alive — its Diablo Canyon nuclear map structured **5,862 arguments from 8,000+ human research hours**, and its "descriptive emergent structuring" method explicitly *discovers the fundamental questions society is arguing about* ([Nuclear Energy Project](https://www.societylibrary.org/nuclear-energy-project), [Debate Maps](https://www.societylibrary.org/debate-mapping-program)).
- **What works:** Society Library's question-first framing independently validates the core 360 insight. Kialo shows students genuinely report perspective-taking gains.
- **What users ignore/mock:** The academic verdict is blunt: argumentation tools "have hardly overcome issues of non-expert users' uptake" ([ACM C&T paper on failures of argumentation tools](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3461564.3461584)). Maps of 5,862 nodes are archives, not products; nobody checks one in 10 seconds. DebateGraph's 2010 prestige partnerships produced no consumer habit.
- **What's missing:** Time. These maps are *synchronic* — no born dates, no prediction outcomes, no deadline slippage, no mutation lineage. An argument map tells you the shape of a debate, never *how each side's claims have aged*. Also: heroic manual cost per map.

### G. Academic controversy mapping — Latour/Venturini, MACOSPOL

- **What they map:** The full actor-network of a technoscientific controversy; MACOSPOL (2008–09) built controversy-atlas websites; taught in ~10 universities ([Venturini, "Diving in Magma"](https://medialab.sciencespo.fr/publications/Venturini-Diving_in_Magma.pdf), [Mapping controversies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mapping_controversies), [Venturini's field guide](http://www.tommasoventurini.it/wp/category/controversy-mapping/)).
- **What works:** Two decades of methodological vocabulary to steal from: controversies as the unit, "observe where reality is still being negotiated," multi-layer atlases, digital-methods harvesting.
- **What's missing/why it stalled:** Deliverables are student websites and PDFs; the method is admittedly under-documented; it describes actor positions but refuses (on theoretical principle — ANT symmetry) to *score* anyone. It is descriptive cartography with no verdict layer, no consumer surface, and no automation. It legitimizes the category intellectually; it competes with nothing.

### H. Deliberation clustering — Polis / vTaiwan

- **What they map:** Live opinion landscapes: ML clusters participants into camps, surfaces consensus vs. divisive statements; vTaiwan used it to structure national AI-regulation debate into five opinion groups ([Metagov on Polis](https://gov-acc.metagov.org/solutions/Polis-Opinion-Clustering), [FAccT 2025 field study](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3715275.3732205), [People Powered case study](https://www.peoplepowered.org/news-content/digital-participation-case-study-taiwan)).
- **What works:** Proof that "camps" can be computed and rendered legibly — direct precedent for Narrative Radar's consensus + contested-camps object.
- **What's missing:** Maps *who believes what right now*, not *which belief is earning or losing its receipts*. Event-scoped (one consultation), not persistent; requires convening participants.

### I. Scientific evidence layers — scite, Consensus, Examine

- **What they map:** scite classifies every citation as supporting/contrasting/mentioning, with the snippet shown ([scite](https://scite.ai/), [QSS paper](https://direct.mit.edu/qss/article/2/3/882/102990/scite-A-smart-citation-index-that-displays-the)). Consensus's Meter shows the yes/no/possibly split across top-20 papers for a question, with quality gates — it even suppresses the meter under 5 classifiable papers ([Consensus Meter](https://help.consensus.app/en/articles/10069920-the-consensus-meter), [Meter 2.0](https://consensus.app/home/blog/new-consensus-meter/)). Examine grades 800+ interventions against 400+ outcomes, A–F per outcome, zero ads/investors ([Examine grades](https://examine.com/about/grades/), [database](https://examine.com/database/)).
- **What works:** These are the closest existing analogs to the Receipt Strip: directional evidence tallies, honesty gates (Consensus's n>=5 rule mirrors Narrative Radar's n>=5 resolved gate almost exactly), per-claim rather than per-entity grading (Examine). Examine is also the *perfect answer-shopping database for the "why am I always tired" example* — but inverted: it maps interventions→outcomes, not question→competing causal stories.
- **What's missing:** They only cover claims that live inside peer-reviewed literature. The narratives Narrative Radar tracks are born on YouTube/X *before* (or without ever) touching a journal — adrenal fatigue was never a real scientific contest; it was a content contest. No predictor identities, no born dates, no clickbait taxonomy, no lineage. Nobody there maps answer-vs-answer; each claim is evaluated in isolation.

### J. Knowledge graphs — Golden, Diffbot

- Golden's "self-constructing knowledge graph" ambition ended in a 2024 acqui-absorption into ComplyAdvantage (AML compliance) despite $60M from a16z ([golden.com](https://golden.com/), [Tracxn](https://tracxn.com/d/companies/golden/__buya2i36ultbS-89db6Qzy7v__SHpsUv_bUwRa4cUWk)). Diffbot's trillion-fact graph maps entities and facts, sold as API infrastructure ([Diffbot KG](https://www.diffbot.com/products/knowledge-graph/)).
- **Lesson:** Entity-and-fact graphs are commodity infrastructure with no consumer pull. A graph of *things* is not a graph of *contested explanations*; the interesting edges (competes, mutates_into) don't exist in their ontologies. Golden's fate is a warning against "map everything" horizontality — Narrative Radar's edge is verticality (contested claims only) plus verdicts.

### K. Enterprise narrative intelligence — Blackbird.AI (also Graphika, PeakMetrics, Cyabra)

- **What they map:** Real-time "narrative attacks" against brands/governments: narrative feeds, actor networks, bot amplification, deepfake detection; Gartner calls Blackbird the category frontrunner ([Blackbird platform](https://blackbird.ai/platform/), [narrative intelligence](https://blackbird.ai/narrative-intelligence/), [Compass launch](https://blackbird.ai/blog/product-launch-compass-by-blackbird-ai-designed-for-navigating-information-overload-and-disinformation/)).
- **What works:** Proves narrative-as-object is a fundable, Gartner-recognized data category, with detection pipelines similar in spirit to Narrative Radar's classifier.
- **What's missing (three structural gaps):** (1) *Defense frame* — narratives are threats to a client, not answers competing for a civilian's question; (2) *no accountability ledger* — they never score whether a narrative's predictions came true, because the client doesn't care; (3) *enterprise pricing* — five-to-six-figure contracts, invisible to the person actually inside the rabbit hole. Blackbird is the strongest *adjacent* player and the least likely to pivot to consumers.

### L. Platform topic surfaces — YouTube info panels, TikTok topic controls

- YouTube's information panels are generic Wikipedia blurbs applied inconsistently (found on only ~16.6% of relevant search pages; users confuse them with ads; YouTube publishes no effectiveness metrics) ([Science Feedback study](https://science.feedback.org/information-panels-youtube-videos-containing-disinformation-quick-check-study/), [ACM longitudinal audit](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3648188.3675128), [Euronews 2025](https://www.euronews.com/next/2025/08/07/youtubes-misinformation-tools-are-applied-inconsistently-throughout-europe-report-warns)). TikTok's "Manage Topics" covers 12 topics — mental health not among them ([Washington Post rabbit-hole investigation](https://www.washingtonpost.com/technology/interactive/2025/tiktok-algorithm-mental-health/)). X's Community Notes: fewer than 10% of proposed notes ever display, and they arrive after the viral window ([arXiv timeliness study](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.12559v1), [coverage/speed deficiencies](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2412.13280)).
- **Lesson:** Platforms attach context to *individual items*, reactively, and users tune it out. None will ever build the map, because the map is an exit ramp from their own engagement loop — this is the moat the brand line ("the algorithm pulls you deeper; this is the exit") already claims.

### M. Cultural proof-of-demand artifacts

- **Abbie Richards' Conspiracy Chart** (2020): a static inverted pyramid ranking conspiracy theories from "grounded in reality" to "antisemitic point of no return" went massively viral and became a meme template ([Boston Globe](https://www.bostonglobe.com/2021/11/29/metro/this-chart-conspiracy-theories-has-gone-viral-freebritney-qanon-local-disinformation-researcher-breaks-down-what-know/), [meme.com](https://meme.com/memes/the-conspiracy-chart)). One static JPEG with an opinionated y-axis got more organic distribution than every argument-mapping platform combined. **Demand signal: people crave ranked maps of contested claims, delivered as a single legible visual with a strong editorial spine.**
- **PhilPapers Survey**: 100 questions, ~1,800 philosophers, positions + correlations; notably found the community *systematically misestimates* which positions are popular ([PhilPapers surveys](https://philpapers.org/surveys/), [Bourget & Chalmers](https://consc.net/papers/survey.pdf)) — evidence that even experts need the camp-map made explicit.
- **AnswerThePublic / AlsoAsked**: SEO tools that visualize the *question space* around a keyword (search wheels, People-Also-Ask trees) ([AlsoAsked review](https://ecommerceparadise.com/alsoasked-review-2026-the-best-tool-for-people-also-ask-research-and-search-intent-mapping/)). They prove questions are harvestable at scale and map beautifully — but they serve marketers *feeding* the content machine. Narrative Radar would be the first to aim question-mapping at the *consumer* of the content machine. These tools are also the obvious pipeline for discovering "fingerprints" ("why am I always tired" is literally a PAA node).

## 2. Cross-cutting patterns

**What works, across everything:**
1. **Editorial spine beats neutral completeness** (Conspiracy Chart, Ground News Blindspot vs. DebateGraph's exhaustive neutrality).
2. **Per-claim verdicts beat per-entity reputation** (Examine grades each benefit separately; scite classifies each citation).
3. **Honesty gates build trust** (Consensus suppressing its meter under n=5 — direct precedent for the Receipt Strip's gating).
4. **The 10-second glance is the product; the depth is the credibility** (Consensus Meter on top, papers underneath).

**Why everything failed or stalled at the actual 360 job:**
1. **Structure without time** (argument maps: no aging, no receipts) or **time without structure** (forecasting: receipts, no map).
2. **Neutrality without verdicts** (controversy cartography, Wikipedia) or **verdicts without coverage** (Rootclaim: a handful of questions).
3. **Manual cost** (Society Library: 8,000 hours per map) — pre-LLM economics. Narrative Radar's paste-a-link auto-classifying engine is precisely the cost collapse that makes maps maintainable.
4. **Wrong customer** (Blackbird: brands; AnswerThePublic: marketers; ACH: analysts). Nobody serves the person inside the rabbit hole.
5. **Accountability with no daily use case dies** (PunditTracker).

## 3. The white space, precisely

No product in 2026 combines ALL of: **(a)** the *question* as the persistent first-class object; **(b)** rival explanations as navigable, comparable nodes (competes / supplements / feeds / shares_clock / mutates_into); **(c)** a per-node accountability receipt (dated predictions by named predictors, resolution status, deadline slippage, recycled share, next falsifiable date); **(d)** temporal lineage (answers succeeding each other over decades for the same demand); **(e)** sourced from the actual battlefield (YouTube/X), not journals; **(f)** consumer-grade 10-second legibility.

The nearest partial overlaps: Society Library has (a)+(b) without (c)(d)(f); Metaculus has (c) without (a-as-question-ecology)(b)(e)(f); scite/Consensus/Examine have (c)-adjacent evidence receipts without (b)(e); Blackbird has (e) without (b)(c)(f) and the wrong customer; Ground News has (f) without (b)(c)(d). **The receipt-bearing map of rival answers is unclaimed. "Comparison shopping for explanations" has no incumbent.**

**Nearest-threat watch:** (1) Perplexity/OpenAI shipping a "compare viewpoints" mode — mitigated by their statelessness: they can generate a comparison but have no persistent ledger, no memory of who predicted what when (Google's two retreats from perspective-surfacing suggest low appetite); (2) Consensus/scite expanding from papers to creators — culturally unlikely; (3) Blackbird consumerizing — contradicts their Gartner-era enterprise trajectory; (4) Society Library adopting LLM automation — closest in philosophy, slowest in productization, nonprofit metabolism.

## 4. Implications for the relation typology v0

- **competes** — independently validated by Society Library's question-first structuring, Rootclaim's hypothesis sets, and ACH. Sound. Consider borrowing ACH's discipline: what evidence would *discriminate* between two competing answers is the highest-value edge annotation.
- **supplements** — no precedent anywhere; this is the most original edge and the most monetization-revealing (NAD+ riding mitochondria). Blackbird tracks amplifiers-of-narratives, the closest cousin, but never the commercial-parasite relation.
- **feeds** — matches controversy cartography's meta-narrative layering; risk: upstream meta-narratives ("modern life is poisoning you") are unfalsifiable, so their receipt strips will be permanently empty. Consider rendering feeds-nodes with a visibly different, "unfalsifiable by construction" treatment rather than an empty receipt.
- **shares_clock** — genuinely novel; Metaculus's conditional questions and question groups are the only precedent, and they never surface it as narrative-to-narrative linkage. Likely the most differentiating edge for finance narratives.
- **mutates_into** — no product precedent at all (only academic genealogy done by hand). Hardest to source automatically, biggest storytelling payoff; the "adrenal fatigue (2000s) → mitochondria (2020s)" arc is exactly what a one-image viral artifact (Conspiracy Chart lesson) could be built from.
- **Missing from v0, observed in the wild:** a *debunks/polices* relation (fact-check and science-communicator content that exists only in opposition to an answer — visible in scite's "contrasting" class and Community Notes) — worth considering as a sixth edge or as a label on competes.
